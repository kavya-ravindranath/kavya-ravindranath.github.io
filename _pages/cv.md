    <h2 id="cv-working-papers">Working Papers</h2>
    <h3><a href="{{ '/assets/pdfs/KavyaRavindranath_JMP.pdf' | relative_url }}">Raising Revenues: Evidence from Property Tax Assessments in India</a> <em>(Job Market Paper)</em></h3>
    <p><strong>Abstract:</strong> How do property owners respond to increases in property-tax liabilities, and how does heterogeneity in these behavioral responses affect the revenue-maximizing tax rate? I construct a novel dataset of geolocated properties and digitized parcel-level maps from an Indian municipality to answer these questions. Exploiting quasi-random variation arising from area-based property assessments, I find that raising liabilities increases total revenue but reduces compliance, with an overall revenue elasticity of 0.85. Compliance responses vary substantially across the liability distribution, property owners initially facing higher liabilities exhibit larger negative responses. Accounting for this heterogeneity halves the revenue-maximizing tax rate, from four times to twice the current rate. Finally, I find that updating area-based assessment to reflect revenue-maximizing rates would lead to large gains in revenue, but simply updating outdated assessments to more recent values would recover a substantial share of these gains.</p>

    <h3>Bridging the Solar Divide: Demand Responses to Subsidies among Low-Income Households</h3>
    <p>with <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/rui-zhong">Rui Zhong</a></p>
    <p><strong>Abstract:</strong> Governments increasingly rely on subsidies to encourage household adoption of clean technology, yet credible estimates of demand elasticities for low-income households, remain limited. In this paper, we study demand responses to solar subsidies in California. Using an RDD, we find a large price elasticity of -1.7.</p>

    <h3>Political Economy of State Borrowing and Financial Crowding Out</h3>
    <p>with <a href="https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/sternfin/vacharya/public_html/~vacharya.htm">Viral Acharya</a>, <a href="https://www.nirupamakulkarni.com/">Nirupama Kulkarni</a>, and Rahul Singh Chauhan</p>
    <p><strong>Abstract:</strong> Using regulatory primary auction data of state government securities, we find states with the same ruling party as the central government increase issuance by 28% at 43 bps lower yields in the year before elections. The crowding out mechanism operates through government-owned banks, who bid 23% higher amounts and at a 70 bps discount relative to other bidders. Consequently, firms with a 10% higher share of PSB borrowing see a 9.3% reduction in debt. Finally, we establish the underlying political economy channel wherein aligned states increase consumption expenditure, which increases the probability of the ruling party getting re-elected.</p>
